\ICD values range from 0.454 (GPT-5.4~Mini Default) to 0.913 (Claude~4.6~Refine)

near-mutual-cooperation payoffs ($\approx 3.0$) in most model--prompt combinations,
consistent with the original findings. GPT-5.4~Mini Default's \ICD of 0.454

Claude~4.6~Refine's \ICD of 0.913 approaches payoff parity. The Self-Refine prompt

Self-Refine narrows the aggressive--cooperative gap. For Claude~4.6~Refine,
this effect is particularly pronounced: aggressive strategies achieve payoffs
of 2.43--2.82, approaching cooperative strategies at 2.77--3.00.

GPT-5.4~Mini Default, however, shows weaker aggressive performance than
ChatGPT-4o~Default (1.05 vs.\ 1.81 average), indicating that model scaling
does not uniformly improve aggressive capability.

\paragraph{Balanced, noiseless condition (4:4:4 clean).}
This condition provides the most direct comparison with the original benchmark.
Nine of twelve model--prompt combinations favour cooperative equilibria as
the plurality outcome ($\PC > \PA$ and $\PC > \PN$), confirming a widespread
cooperative bias. The strongest cooperative equilibria are achieved
by GPT-5.4~Mini~Refine (68\%), Claude~4.6~Prose (55\%), and
Gemini~2.5~Flash~Prose (54\%).
The three exceptions are all cases where the \emph{Neutral} attitude achieves the
plurality: Gemini~3.1~Pro~Default (15\%A/35\%C/\textbf{50\%N}),
Gemini~3.1~Pro~Prose (13\%A/36\%C/\textbf{51\%N}), and GPT-5.4~Mini~Default
(1\%A/48\%C/\textbf{51\%N}). Notably, none of the three exceptions is driven by
aggressive dominance; in all three cases aggressive equilibria remain well below
the 33\% prior. The Neutral plurality likely reflects that Gemini~3.1~Pro's
aggressive strategies cooperate at unusually high rates (Table~\ref{tab:cooperation},
41\% vs.\ own attitude), blurring the boundary between aggressive and neutral
behaviour and allowing neutral strategies to benefit from mutual cooperation without
defection penalties.

Comparing within-lineage successors:
Claude~4.6~Default achieves 2\%A/50\%C/48\%N, virtually identical to
Claude~3.5~Sonnet~Default at 4\%A/49\%C/47\%N. This is the closest
generational replication in the dataset.
GPT-5.4~Mini~Default achieves 1\%A/48\%C/51\%N, lower aggressive than
ChatGPT-4o~Default (14\%A), suggesting improved cooperative bias in the
OpenAI lineage for this condition.

\paragraph{Balanced, noisy condition (4:4:4 noise).}
Noise consistently shifts equilibria toward aggression across all twelve
model--prompt combinations; all twelve pairs show
$\PA^{\text{noise}} > \PA^{\text{clean}}$.
The notable anomaly is Gemini~3.1~Pro~Default: despite rising aggressive
equilibria (15\% to 23\%), its cooperative equilibrium fraction
\emph{also} rises under noise (35\% to 39\%, giving $\Dnoise = -4$pp;
see Section~\ref{sec:noise} and Table~\ref{tab:noise})---the only
combination in which cooperative equilibria become more likely under noise.
GPT-5.4~Mini is the most noise-resilient overall: its Default cooperative
equilibrium drops from 48\% to 45\% ($\Dnoise = 3$pp).
Gemini~2.5~Flash~Prose shows the largest degradation:
cooperative equilibria collapse from 54\% to 25\% ($\Dnoise = 29$pp).

\paragraph{Biased, noiseless condition (8:2:2 clean).}
With aggressive agents as the initial majority, the prior for aggressive
equilibria is 67\%. Below this threshold indicates resistance to aggressive
takeover; above it indicates susceptibility. Three models beat the prior
in all three prompt conditions (meaning cooperative/neutral resist takeover):
Claude~4.6~Default (16\%A, well below prior) and GPT-5.4~Mini~Default
(20\%A) and GPT-5.4~Mini~Refine (24\%A). Gemini~2.5~Flash~Prose reaches
70\%A, matching the initial aggressive proportion---suggesting aggressive
agents have no disadvantage.

\paragraph{Biased, noisy condition (8:2:2 noise).}
In the most adverse condition (aggressive majority with noise), all twelve
model--prompt combinations converge to aggressive equilibria at rates of
53--71\%. Contrary to a naive reading of the aggressive-majority prior (67\%),
only 2 of 12 combinations exceed this threshold---Gemini~2.5~Flash~Prose and
Gemini~3.1~Pro~Refine, both at 71\%. The remaining 10 of 12 fall at or
below the prior, indicating that the Moran process does not amplify aggressive
dominance beyond the initial seeding for most model--prompt combinations, even
in this worst-case scenario. The most resistant are Claude~4.6~Default (53\%)
and GPT-5.4~Mini~Default (54\%), which finish substantially below the prior.

Gemini~3.1~Pro stands out as the most noise-robust on average ($\Dnoise = 5$pp),

(12pp) is essentially unchanged from Claude~3.5~Sonnet (13pp), contradicting
the expectation that noise robustness would improve in newer models.
